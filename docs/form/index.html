What bothers you that isn’t in your records?

This is the part your records cannot answer. The conditions service members most often go uncompensated for are the ones nobody ever wrote down.

Nothing here checks a box on its own.

Whatever you tick becomes a proposal on the next screen, worded the way the form words it, and you confirm it there like everything else. Ticking “ringing in my ears” is not the same statement as “a hearing loss” — you should see the sentence you are actually signing.

Tick anything that is true for you, whether or not you ever reported it. Never going to sick call is ordinary, and it is not evidence that nothing was wrong. Skip the whole screen if nothing applies.

What to do next

  1. Do not sign anything yet. Both forms are unsigned on purpose. Sign them after your VSO has read them, not before.
  2. Fill in your SSN and DoD ID by hand. This tool never collects them, so both are blank in eight boxes across four places: DD 2807-1 items 2a and 2b on page 1, the header of page 2, the header of page 3, and section 1 of SHA Part A.
  3. Finish Item 29.
  4. Book a free accredited VSO. They are free, they do this daily, and they are the ones accredited to advise on what to claim. Find one at VA’s directory.
  5. Bring: this whole package, your DD-214, your complete service treatment records, and any private medical records VA does not already hold.
  6. Ask them: which conditions are worth claiming, whether any are presumptive for your service, whether anything is missing, and — if you are still serving — whether you are inside the 180-to-90-day BDD window.
The conditions worksheet is the part to hand over first.

It lists everything found in your records with the page it came from, including the conditions this tool could not match to a form question. That list is exactly what a VSO needs to spot what you have missed.
